# Escalation script formula (no room to scroll)

Source: study of 6 hit shorts on 2026-11-04 (Ricks Clips: Ancient Jar 31M, Perfect Railroads 18.7M, Job Nobody Wants
16.4M; MrPasserby: Movers 4.3M, Seed 1.8M, Paradox of the Court 0.6M), compared with our two uploads that stalled at
~2k (Newton coin clipping 90 s, Edo firefighters vs sumo 71 s). This formula **replaces** the bridge beat formula
(setting → explanation with an analogy → consequence) and the "You see → mechanism → analogy" part of STYLE_BIBLE.md.
Study material only: never copy their stories, lines or shots. Our stories and wording are always our own.

## The one idea
The viewer must always be waiting for something. The hook opens one big question, and it is answered only in the
**last line**. In between, every 5-7 s something new happens that makes the situation worse, bigger, or more
humiliating, and opens a smaller question before the last one closes. There is never a sentence where nothing happens.

## Shape (55-65 s, 170-220 words)

| Time | Beat | What it does | Hit examples (paraphrased) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0-3 s | **Hook** | One person does something that sounds stupid or impossible, or "you" are put in a strange situation. It hides a promise that only the end pays off. | inventor puts a ball in a dragon's mouth and tells the emperor to run if it falls · engineer saws gaps into a brand-new railroad · "you can hire movers to make you vanish" |
| 3-10 s | **Stakes + doubt** | What's at risk, and someone laughs, doubts or refuses. One short quoted line. | the court bursts out laughing · boss's face goes purple: "You're wrecking my railroad" |
| 10-45 s | **Escalation ladder** (4-6 rungs) | Each rung is a new event, 5-8 s long, bigger than the last: costlier, more public, more humiliating, closer to disaster. The hero looks more and more wrong. | a ball falls and nothing happens, so they laugh harder → he's thrown out of the city → he lives alone on a hill → all eight balls drop at night → the guards laugh him back up the hill |
| ~35-45 s | **Lowest point** | The hero is at maximum humiliation, right before the turn. | kid crying behind a pot of dirt · "Did your frogs get hungry again?" |
| 45-58 s | **Reversal** | The disaster or judgement lands; everyone who laughed is wrong. | the palace is rubble by morning · the perfect railroad kicks sideways like a snake |
| last 3-5 s | **Reveal = punchline** | The hidden mechanism or the twist, said as the **last** line. The video ends on that word, or mid-sentence so it loops into line 1. | "...right underneath them." · "...every seed was boiled." · "...which is why 2,400 years later," → loops back to the hook |

## Hard rules
1. **One protagonist.** At most 3 speaking roles: hero, mocker, authority. Everyone else is "the crowd", "the guards", "the whole court".
2. **No names, years or place names in the first 10 s.** Use roles ("the emperor", "the youngest engineer", "your boss").
   Use at most one proper name in the whole video, and only if the name *is* the twist (e.g. a famous person revealed
   at the end). Put real names, dates and sources in the description.
3. **Something new happens in every sentence.** If a sentence only sets the scene or explains, cut it or merge it into an action.
   Never more than 2 sentences in a row without a turn word that changes the situation ("Then", "But", "So", "Meanwhile", "Until").
4. **Always keep a question open.** Open the next question *before* closing the current one
   ("Weeks later, a ball fell. But nothing happened." opens "so is he a fraud?").
5. **No lecture section and no analogy section.** The cause is shown through events. The full "why" arrives only in the last 10 s, as the payoff.
   The old "Luckily / Unluckily" double line and a mid-video "every bridge has a rhythm..." explanation are the exact drop-off points.
6. **Escalate on three axes at once:** stakes (money/lives/throne), scale (one ball → all eight; one kid → thousands of flowers),
   and humiliation (laughed at → thrown out → mocked at the gates). Each rung raises at least one.
7. **Mockery is the engine.** 2-4 short in-character lines of doubt or mockery. The viewer stays to see the mockers proven wrong.
   Our rule still holds: jokes and doubts must be true or a fair reading of the real situation. Never invent false history.
   If the real story has no mocker, use the real expectation ("Everyone expects him to sign some papers and go home").
8. **1-2 exaggeration jokes**, short, inside an action line ("the longest conversation anyone's ever had with dirt").
9. **The ending is the biggest moment.** Never end on a moral, an afterthought, "so now...", or a fact about today.
   Loop option: cut mid-sentence so the last words run into the hook.
10. **Length 55-65 s.** One story per video. A second act ("But catching crooks wasn't enough...") is a new video.
11. **Voice:** present tense, contractions, spoken English, sentences ≤ 16 words; "You see," at most once, and only right before the reveal.

## Before / after from our own uploads
- **Coin clipping (90 s, ~2k views).** Hook gave away the answer ("the ridges are there because Newton..."), 1696 in
  line 2, two stories (the forger hunt, then the coin redesign), pie-crust analogy at 70 s, quiet last line.
  Rewrite the opening as: *A man walks into the roughest pub in London and asks who's been shaving the King's coins.
  The whole pub laughs. He's the most famous scientist alive.* One story (the hunt), his name only in the last line.
- **Edo firefighters (71 s, ~1.4k views).** "In 1805" first, about 8 groups of characters, 13 s of world-building before anything
  happens, last line an afterthought. The best material (a city punishing a *bell*) should be the hook's promise:
  *A city once put a bell on trial, and the bell lost.* Then one fireman's free ticket starts the ladder.
- **Bridge (template).** Strong hook, but the swing analogy at ~45 s is a lecture and the end is a soft rule.
  Under this formula the mockery is the soldiers' own (they found the bounce funny and stomped harder, which is true),
  the rungs are the bounce getting bigger, the snap is the reversal, and the resonance idea becomes the final line.
